# The World's Power Curve: Access Items, Dungeons and Pearls

*Status: a proposal for the owner, on branch `exp/body-shapes` only. Written 2026-10-11 from three idea sets (Zelda-faithful, slime-body, pacing and story) and a judge, after the owner asked whether a dungeon should give two powers and asked for ideas on the item that opens each region's dungeon. Goes with the [body-shapes plan](2026-10-11-body-shapes-plan.md) and the [Release 1 dungeons plan](2026-10-11-release-1-dungeons-plan.md). Names of new characters, places and bosses are proposals.*

## The owner's answers (2026-10-11)

- **Powers per region follow the story.** If the story needs it, a region may give three powers and
  another only one; Zelda gives three or four in the first chapter because the player needs them
  to progress. The "one new power per region" rule below is a guide, not a law.
- **The War Axe** (it was the Sword) stays the Chapter 2 dungeon's item, found halfway through the
  Weaver Hollow. Nothing else is added there, so that dungeon gives one item.
- **Pearl Gifts** (upgrades or new moves): not decided yet. The owner will decide later; the
  rework is built first and tested in the playground.
- **The Roll in Chapter 6:** fine for now.
- **The step after Tom: option B, Kiri's Three Marks.** The three pieces are put together at one
  place, and in a short cinematic the door rises out of the ground.

**My pick: Set 3 as the base, with parts of the other two.**
- From Set 2: the lantern is an ember inside the slime, and box powers cost energy, not ammo.
- From Set 1: the Belfry and Glint, the rule "one power per kind of obstacle", and the mouth's last blow on Gorge.

**Short answer to your question: no, a dungeon should not give two new abilities.**
- Each region gives one new power.
- In most regions this power is the item that opens the dungeon, the idea you liked.
- The Pearl gives a full heart and an upgrade of a power the slime already has.

## 1. The reward rule

- **A region** gives one new power. Usually it is the access item. A local character teaches it outside the dungeon, in a small cave or a short quest, and it opens the dungeon door.
- **A dungeon** gives no new power. It holds small keys, the map, the big key and treasure, and its second half shows a new use of the region's power (a "twist"). There are three exceptions, because these powers are too strong to carry to the door: the Axe (your decision), the Hammer and the Lash. Each one lies in the big chest halfway through its dungeon, and older powers open that door.
- **A Pearl** gives a full heart (+20), a piece of the story and a **Pearl Gift**. A Pearl Gift makes the slime or an old power stronger, or shows more secrets. It is never a new key or box socket, and the slime uses it at once in the Pearl room. Pearl 1's Squash Slam is the one exception.

**The other rule: two powers per dungeon** (an item found inside, plus a Pearl that teaches a new move). Why not:
- 8 dungeons would give 16 or more powers.
- Good ideas and new systems would run out by about Chapter 6.
- The 8-socket box would overflow.
- Every dungeon would have to teach two things.
- Late chapters would feel like "one more gadget" instead of a new place.

## 2. What changes in Chapters 1 and 2

Very little. Release 1 stays as planned, with these changes:

- **Spear:** stays the same. Yoi teaches it in Yoi's Hideout, and it has its Lunge charge from the start.
  - New: the burrow's plank barricade hangs on a stone eye-latch over the door. One Spear thrust drops the planks.
  - Worm Trouble still sends the slime there (Mossy: "They said it opens to a point."). But now the region's power opens the dungeon, not a quest flag.
- **Squash Slam:** stays the same. Pearl 1 teaches it (Ability Box, S). It is the only Pearl that adds a new move, because the slime starts with only a mouth.
- **Swallow:** stays the same. Tom teaches it after the Spear frees him. It uses your new Q rule:
  - one press sucks for one second;
  - a tap with something in the mouth throws it or spits it;
  - holding Q for 2 s swallows an essence, or spits out the current form.

  It counts as an upgrade of Q, not a new power. The Silk form is the way into the Weaver Hollow, through the web door.
- **Axe:** stays the same. It lies in the Edge Shrine's big chest, halfway through the Weaver Hollow. Its Spin is its charge from the start.
- **Pearl 2:** the Spin is gone, so Pearl 2 gives **Silk Reel** (the Matron's silk in the mouth).
  - The one-second suck reaches 160 px instead of 80.
  - It pulls light things over water and holes, and through bars.
  - First use: Sunny's basket across the north stream (1696, 168). Her parked quest ends right after Pearl 2.
  - To build: one constant and a rule for grates.
- **Teasers for later powers.** Each one has a refusal line:
  - Dry deadthorn hedges close level-1's west and north exits: "Dry as bone. Fire would take it." (Lantern, Chapter 3). They replace invisible walls.
  - The plaza drawbridge has been raised since the war (Boomerang, Chapter 4).
  - Cold worm candles stand behind a dry-thorn plug in the lair's shrine (Lantern).
  - An iron-banded keg: "Too heavy to suck in. Not yet." (Pearl 3).
  - Stone and iron nodes: "A hammer could crack this." (Chapter 7).
- **End of Chapter 2:** Pip gives the slime "Dad's coal", the Forge's last coal, which he kept alive in a jar. It is the story key for Chapter 3.
- Release 1's box holds only the Slam, so the R key is never taught in Release 1.

**Your answer 6, if it was about the step after Tom.** Three options:
- **A. The Sealed Hollow (default).** The Stone form presses the plate (2880, 3016). Kiri's cache holds the map and his note: "my blade sleeps below the stump". It is one quest, so the busiest chapter stays short.
- **B. Kiri's Three Marks.** The hollow, the silk nook and the Gloop Cavern each hold a third of Kiri's note. Together they show the stump's door. It is longer, and it puts the Cavern on the main path.
- **C. Tom's Bottle.** Tom asks the slime to carry a silk essence in the bag past the orb-weavers to the web door. It teaches the bag trick that the Weigh Room needs.

## 4. The lantern

**Where:** Chapter 3. It is the Crystal Caverns' access item, from the smith.
- It is not in Fatty's Lair. There, Chapter 1 would teach three powers and put dark rooms into the first dungeon.
- Release 1 only teases it: the worm candles and the deadthorn hedges.

**What it is:** the Forge's ember, kept alive inside the slime. The slime glows like a jelly lantern. The ember lives in the body, so it survives forms and hits, and the rule "the slime never holds anything" stays true. It sits in the Ability Box and is used with S.

**How it works:**
- **In dark rooms:** a light circle of about 160 px around the slime, for free.
- **Tap S:** a short flame puff about one tile ahead (about 5 energy). It lights or sets fire to what it touches.
- **Hold S** on frost or ice: it melts slowly (about 1.5 s).
- **Long range:** the slime sucks a lit coal or a candle jar (both liftables exist) and throws it, or the boomerang carries the flame (Chapter 4).

**Candles, torches and braziers:**
- They are strike switches with a new `fire` tag and a fifth look.
- They can stay lit for good (`opens_for_good`).
- They can burn for a few seconds (`open_ms`), so the slime must light all four before the first one burns out.
- They can belong to a `group` that opens a shutter.
- A dark room whose candles all burn becomes lit.
- Rain and splashes put candles out (Swamp).
- In the Caverns, dark-crystal walls recede while a nearby brazier burns.

**Burnable obstacles: only dry, dead or frozen things.**
- Deadthorn hedges (Gorge's black blight)
- Hay heaps and bales
- Plank barricades and rotten mine timbers
- Dry root plugs
- Wax seals and old banners
- Frost on locks

How fire behaves:
- A burnable burns in about 0.6 s and spreads to burnables it touches, so a hedge burns end to end.
- Fire also runs along placed fuses, so one puff can light a brazier across water.
- Fire never spreads to grass or living trees.
- Burnt things stay burnt (a saved flag).

**How it differs from the Axe:**

| | War Axe | Lantern |
|---|---|---|
| Works on | Living green things and silk: webs, thick webs, vines, bushes, trees, tethers, cocoons | Dead, dry or frozen things, and lights |
| How | Instant, at reach; deals damage; gives wood | Takes time; spreads; deals no damage (light-shy creatures flee) |
| Refusal line | On deadthorn: "Too dry and hard. Fire would take it." | On a web or a bush: "Too green and wet to burn." |

The rule for the player: **green is cut, dry burns, cold melts.** Webs stay Axe-only, so the Silk and Axe puzzles keep working.

**To build:**
- The `fire` tag and a lit look on the strike switch.
- A `burnable.gd` in the `tom_web.gd` pattern, with a saved flag.
- Fuses chained by an export list (no tile fire).
- One darkness mask drawn in code, with holes for the slime and the lit candles. No 2D light per candle, which is too slow on WebGL 2. Prototype it in the playground and test it on the web export early.

## 5. The full power curve

NEW = a new power. UP = an upgrade of something the slime already has.

- **Ch 1 Slimeshire:** NEW Spear (Yoi; access). NEW Squash Slam (Pearl 1, the exception).
- **Ch 2 Gloop Forest:** UP Swallow (Tom; access). NEW Axe (found halfway). UP Silk Reel (Pearl 2).
- **Ch 3 Crystal Caverns:** NEW Lantern (the smith; access). UP Heavy Gulp I (Pearl 3).
- **North Wilds:** nothing new; extra heart quarters.
- **Ch 4 Burrowby:** NEW Goo Boomerang (Wren; access). UP Magpie's Eye (Pearl 4). Midpoint: fast travel opens.
- **South Wilds:** nothing new; extra heart quarters.
- **Ch 5 Sticky Swamp:** NEW Goo Bombs, with spores (Mother Morel; access). UP the Swim form gets its source enemy (the Mudpuppy; a small "New form" card). UP Deep Dive (Pearl 5).
- **Ch 6 Desert:** NEW Roll (Tumble; access). UP Heavy Gulp II (Pearl 6).
- **Ch 7 Frostpeak:** NEW Hammer (Grum; found halfway). UP Thick Jelly (Pearl 7). Pip's Forge opens and tempers the shapes.
- **Ch 8 Northern ruins:** NEW Stretch Lash (found halfway). UP Ember Skin (Pearl 8).
- **Finale:** nothing new.

**In all:** 9 new powers and 10 upgrades.
- 3 shapes, switched with E: Spear, War Axe, Hammer.
- 5 box powers, used with S: Slam, Lantern, Boomerang, Goo Bombs, Lash. Five sockets fit as one short row in the bag window (your idea 8).
- The Roll, on its own key D.

The Roll comes late, in Chapter 6 (Sets 2 and 3 both put it in the Desert). Fast travel from Chapter 4 keeps the walking short.

## 6. The best ideas from the other sets

**From Set 1 (Zelda-faithful):**
- One power per kind of obstacle:
  - cracked ground: Stone-form Slam;
  - cracked walls: Goo Bombs (drop the Stone-form Roll idea, open question 26);
  - rocks, iron and armour: the Hammer;
  - dry and frozen things: the Lantern;
  - green things and silk: the Axe.
- Heavy liftables are iron-banded, so they never look like round boulders.
- The Belfry, Glint the Magpie Queen, and Magpie's Eye.
- Fire carried by the boomerang, and fuses chained by an export list.
- Gorge's last blow is the mouth: the slime sucks the Pearls out of him.
- The Forge and the Workshop become upgrade shops (tempered shapes, a longer lash). They never give new powers.
- Steady Form (a form survives its first hit), kept as a spare Forge upgrade.
- A hit on a swimming slime over deep water sends it back to the last shore.
- Each passage lock stays closed in `world-map.json` until it is built (the atlas session's file).

**From Set 2 (slime-body):**
- The region's own power opens the door, not a quest flag (the Spear eye-latch).
- The lantern is an ember inside the body. Box powers cost energy, not ammo.
- Silk Reel as Pearl 2, with Sunny's basket as its first use. Heavy Gulp I as Pearl 3.
- Release 1 teasers with refusal lines.
- A small "New form" card for the first swim.
- Parked for later: Second Stomach (keep a second form inside), and the Bud (a bud of the slime that it swaps places with), if Teleport ever comes back.
